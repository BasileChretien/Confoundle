Confoundle

What we store, and why

Confoundle works without an account. If you never sign in, this app sends nothing about you anywhere, and there is no record of you to speak of.

The short version

  • No account: your streak, your answers and your review schedule live in your own browser's storage. They never leave the device. There is nothing for us to look at, hand over or lose.
  • With an account: we store your email address and your spaced-repetition schedule, so your reviews follow you between devices. That is the entire list.
  • No advertising, no profiling, no cookies, and nothing that follows you from one site to another, and nothing is ever sold or shared for marketing. Page visits are counted in aggregate, with no identifier attached to them at all: see how visits are counted.
  • You can delete everything yourself, from inside the app, immediately, with no request and no waiting period.

What is deliberately not collected

No name, no profile picture, no contacts, no location, no device fingerprint, no advertising identifier, no behavioural profile. Your streak and your score stay on your own device even when you are signed in: they are not part of what an account syncs, and they are not reported anywhere.

Your answers are the one exception, and it is a narrow one, set out in full below. They are counted, never stored against you.

How answers are counted

Every puzzle tells you what most people did. That number has to come from somewhere, so when you commit to an answer this site sends three things and nothing else:

  • which puzzle it was;
  • which of the options you picked;
  • how sure you said you were.

Only your first answer to a puzzle is sent. Replaying it after the reveal sends nothing, because the number is meant to say what people picked when the figures were new to them, and answers from players who have just been told the answer would drag it towards the correct option and make the deck look easier than it is.

There is no identifier of any kind in that message. No account id, no device id, no cookie, no session, no timestamp beyond the day. It is added to a running tally and the message itself is discarded. Nothing links one of your answers to another, which means no history of your play exists on the server even in principle, and none could be reconstructed later.

What this does not let us do. We cannot tell how you did overall, cannot tell whether two answers came from the same person, and cannot single you out. That is not a promise about how we behave. It is that the data needed to do it is never created.

The honest limit. Like every request to this site, the one carrying your answer reaches Cloudflare, which sees the connection and therefore your IP address in order to route it. That address is not stored with the answer and is not part of the tally. One value derived from it is stored, briefly and separately: so that a script cannot rewrite these numbers by submitting answers in bulk, the site counts how many arrive from one connection each hour. What it keeps for that is a one-way hash of the address under a secret key, in a table holding a count and a start time and nothing else, with no answer and no puzzle beside it. It cannot be turned back into an address, nothing else reads it, and it is overwritten as the hour rolls. But while this site is small, a request is close in time to a visit, and we will not pretend that somebody holding both sets of server logs could never line them up. What we can say precisely is that we do not do that, we keep nothing that would make it easy, and the tally itself contains nothing to link.

You can turn it off. There is a switch in the app, and turning it off stops the message being sent at all rather than asking us to ignore it once it arrives. The puzzles work exactly the same either way, and you will still be told what most people did, from everybody else's answers.

How visits are counted

This site uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count how many people arrive, which pages they land on, and roughly where in the world and on what kind of device. Together with the answer tally described above and the step counter described below, it is the whole of the measurement on this site, and it exists to answer one question the project genuinely needs an answer to: whether any of this is being read.

What that means in practice, stated precisely:

  • It sets no cookie and writes nothing to your browser's storage.
  • It builds no identifier for you, neither a visitor id nor a device fingerprint, so it cannot tell a returning reader from a new one, and neither can we.
  • It works on this site only. There is no way for it, or for us, to see anything you do anywhere else.
  • It reports page addresses, referrer, country, browser and device type, in aggregate. Nothing you type, answer or choose in the app is ever sent to it.
  • It cannot be connected to your account, because nothing identifying goes into it in the first place.

Because it stores nothing on your device and cannot single you out, it needs no consent banner under the ePrivacy rules, and we would rather show you this paragraph than a popup. If you would prefer not to be counted at all, any content blocker will stop the beacon and the site works exactly the same without it. The measurement is processed by Cloudflare on our behalf, under the same processor arrangement as the hosting described below.

How steps of a puzzle are counted

This page used to say that the app carried an internal event module which sent nothing anywhere, and that if it ever started sending, this page would change with it in the same commit. It started sending, and this is that change.

The app now counts which of eight named steps of a puzzle was reached, so the project can see where people stop reading. That is the one question it cannot answer any other way, and the reason it matters is ordinary: work gets spent on the beat that is losing people rather than on the one that felt worth improving.

What is sent, and it is the whole list:

  • Which step: one of puzzle_view, commit, reveal_view, lesson_view, share_open, share_caption_select, share_export, replay. The server refuses any other name.
  • Which puzzle it happened in.

That is all. No identifier, no session, no clock time, no ordering. The date is added by the server, to the day and no finer. Because there is no session, it is not possible to learn from this that one person reached the commit step and then the reveal: it counts steps, never journeys. That is weaker than the funnel an analytics product would give, deliberately, because a session identifier is exactly the field that would turn these rows into a record of somebody's evening.

It goes to this project's own database, in the same Cloudflare account as everything else described here, and never to an analytics company. There is no third-party script on this site for it, nothing to block, and nobody new to trust. The counter obeys the same switch as the answer tally above, described there, which is off in one place for both.

Like the answer tally, it is not personal data as a property of its table rather than as a promise about our behaviour: there is no column that could hold anything about you, so an erasure request has nothing to erase in it.

Email we send you

There are exactly two kinds, and there will never be a third.

  • The sign-in code, sent because you just asked to sign in. It carries no unsubscribe link because there is no list to leave.
  • Review reminders, sent only if you switched them on in the account panel. At most one a day, and only when a review has actually been overdue for at least half a day. If you have used the app in the last day you get nothing, because you already know.

Every reminder carries a one-click unsubscribe link that works without signing in, and your mail app's own "unsubscribe" button works too. Switching them off changes nothing else: your account, your schedule and your progress are untouched. There is no marketing email, no newsletter, no "we miss you" campaign, and no announcement list. The reminder is the only recurring message that exists, and its entire text is in this repository.

Signing in with Google

Google's sign-in script is loaded only when you open the account panel on a deployment that offers it. If you never do, this app never contacts Google at all. There is no automatic prompt.

If you do use it, Google hands the app a signed token identifying you. We check that token, take the account identifier and the email address from it, and discard the rest. We ask for no permissions on your Google account, receive no access token, and can read nothing else from it. In that exchange Google acts as an independent controller under its own privacy policy.

Where the data sits, and who else touches it

  • Cloudflare hosts the site and the database, as a processor acting on our instructions. Like any web host it briefly sees the usual request metadata, including network addresses, under its own policy.
  • The email provider configured for this deployment delivers the sign-in code, as a processor. It sees the address the code is sent to.
  • Google, only if you choose the Google button, as described above.

These services operate globally, so data may be processed outside the European Economic Area under the transfer safeguards in their own terms. Nobody else receives any of it, and none of it is sold, rented or used for advertising.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you may ask for access to your data, correction of it, erasure, portability, restriction, and you may object to processing based on legitimate interests. Two of those are wired into the app so you do not have to ask anyone:

  • Take a copy. Open the account panel and choose Download my data. The file contains everything the server holds about you, in a format the app itself can read back.
  • Erase everything. Open the account panel and choose Delete my account. This removes your account, your reviews and your sessions immediately, on the server and on the device you are using. There is no soft delete, no grace period and no way for us to restore it afterwards.

For anything else, write to the address above. If you are in the EU or EEA you may also complain to your national data protection authority; in France that is the CNIL. Users in Japan have the equivalent rights of disclosure, correction and deletion under the Act on the Protection of Personal Information, and may contact the Personal Information Protection Commission.

Children

Confoundle is not aimed at children and asks for no age information. Please do not create an account if you are under 16, or under the age your country sets for consenting to online services. The app itself is fully playable without one.

Security

There is no password to steal: we store no password hashes, because signing in means proving you control an email address rather than remembering a secret. Sign-in tokens and email codes are stored only as one-way digests, so a copy of the database would not let anyone sign in as you. Traffic is encrypted in transit.
