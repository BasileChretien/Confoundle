# What political and civic education actually names, and what Confoundle covers

Working document, opened 2026-07-29. The companion to
[`exam-syllabus-audit.md`](./exam-syllabus-audit.md), which audits the deck
against the four medical blueprints. This one asks the same question of the
official documents that govern political and civic education, because the
persuasion track has been growing on intuition and needed the same discipline.

The medical audit could point at a licensing exam. Political science has no
licensing exam anywhere, so the analogue is the document that actually binds a
teacher: a national curriculum annexe, a ministry subject-content requirement,
or an examination board's course framework. Those are what is read here.

The same two tiers are kept apart as in the medical audit:

- **Named**: the official document prints the term.
- **Taught**: the concept is standard in teaching but the official document does
  not enumerate it.

## Status

| Jurisdiction | Document | Read | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Japan | 高等学校学習指導要領（平成30年告示）解説 公民編, MEXT, July 2018 | done | Names nothing. The strongest negative in either audit. |
| France | Programmes de spécialité SES, première and terminale, annexes to the arrêtés of 17-1-2019 and 19-7-2019 | done | The richest positive. Names polling, and names the reflexive problem with it. |
| USA | AP U.S. Government and Politics Course and Exam Description, effective Fall 2026 | done | Names polling error in detail, and gerrymandering, and no general statistics. |
| UK | Politics, GCE AS and A level subject content, DfE, May 2022 | done | Names nothing. Identical in shape to the medical finding. |
| Germany, Korea, China, Russia, Spain | not yet read | **pending** | See "What has not been read" at the end. |

Four of nine. This document is a first pass, not the finished audit, and the
gap is stated rather than glossed.

## Japan: a compulsory new civics subject that names no error at all

Since 2022 every Japanese high school student takes 公共 (Kōkyō), a compulsory
subject created by the 2018 revision and explicitly about acting as a citizen.
If any national curriculum were going to name reasoning traps, this is where it
would be.

Source: the MEXT commentary 高等学校学習指導要領（平成30年告示）解説 公民編
(July 2018), which covers 公共, 倫理 and 政治・経済 together. The full text was
extracted and searched, roughly 844,000 characters.

**Counts, from the whole document.**

| Term | Count |
|---|---:|
| 誤謬 (fallacy) | 0 |
| バイアス (bias) | 0 |
| 詭弁 (sophistry) | 0 |
| 印象操作 (impression manipulation) | 0 |
| 世論操作 (manipulation of public opinion) | 0 |
| 相関 (correlation) | 0 |
| 標本 / 標本調査 (sample / sampling) | 0 |
| 平均 / 中央値 (mean / median) | 0 |
| フェイク (fake) | 0 |
| メディア・リテラシー | 1 |
| 批判的 (critical) | 2 |
| 因果関係 (causal relationship) | 1 |
| 統計 (statistics) | 13 |

**And the four non-zero terms do less than the counts suggest.**

- **メディア・リテラシー appears once**, as one item in a list, in the 公共
  section on democratic politics: 「メディア・リテラシーなど，主権者として良識
  ある公正な判断力等を身に付けることが民主政治にとって必要であること」. It is
  named as a thing a sovereign citizen needs. It is never unpacked.
- **批判的 appears twice**, and both times as 批判的に吟味する, examining one's
  **own** reasoning. Once about ancient Greek philosophy
  (「自分の考えを論理的，批判的に吟味する力」) and once about ethical judgement
  (「自らの考えを批判的に吟味することが大切である」). Neither is about
  evaluating a claim somebody else is making.
- **因果関係 appears once, and not as a reasoning concept.** It is in an appendix
  table of information-handling skills, as a way of arranging material:
  「順序や因果関係などで整理して年表にまとめる」, organise by sequence or causal
  relation into a chronological table.
- **統計 appears 13 times**, invariably as material to teach *with*
  (「各種の統計，年鑑，白書，新聞」), never as something that can mislead.

**The nearest thing to Confoundle's subject in the entire document** is a
general instruction to check 出典 (the source), 信頼性 (reliability), 客観性
(objectivity) and 真偽 (truth or falsity), and to consider the
発信者の立場や意図, the position and intent of whoever sent the information.
That is source evaluation, which is a different skill from spotting a flaw in an
argument whose sources are all impeccable. Confoundle's whole premise is the
second one.

This is the same finding shape as the medical audit's Japan entry, and stronger:
there, seven error terms were named. Here, zero.

## France: the only document that names the reflexive problem

France is the richest of the four, and the only one that names something
Confoundle does not already teach.

Sources, both read at source as the annexes to their arrêtés:

- **Première**, arrêté du 17-1-2019, BO spécial n°1 du 22 janvier 2019
  (`spe639_annexe_1063544.pdf`).
- **Terminale**, arrêté du 19-7-2019, BO spécial n°8 du 25 juillet 2019
  (`spe253_annexe_1158821.pdf`).

### What is named

**Public opinion and polling, première, chapter "Comment se forme et s'exprime
l'opinion publique ?".** Two objectives, quoted in full:

> Comprendre les principes et les techniques des sondages, et les débats
> relatifs à leur interprétation de l'opinion publique.

> Comprendre comment le recours fréquent aux sondages d'opinion contribue à
> forger l'opinion publique et modifie l'exercice de la démocratie (démocratie
> d'opinion) et de la vie politique (contrôle des gouvernants, participation
> électorale, communication politique).

**The second one is the find.** France officially teaches that frequent polling
helps *make* the opinion it claims to measure. That is a measurement instrument
changing the thing it measures, which is a reasoning trap of exactly the shape
this deck is built from, and Confoundle has nothing on it.

**Crime statistics, première, chapter on deviance.** One objective:

> Comprendre et illustrer les difficultés de mesure de la délinquance.

That is detection and reporting bias, named, applied to crime rather than to
medicine. Confoundle has `detection-bias`, on a medical case.

**Quantitative objectives, both years.** These are bare lists of tools, but they
are graded into two tiers, and the grading is informative.

- Première, under *Calcul, lecture, interprétation*: proportion, taux de
  variation, indice simple, **moyenne arithmétique simple et pondérée**.
- Première, under *Lecture et interprétation*: indice synthétique, **médiane**,
  valeur nominale et réelle, tableau à double entrée, représentations graphiques.
- Terminale adds, under *Lecture et interprétation*: écart et rapport
  inter-quantile, coefficient de Gini, **corrélation et causalité**, taux de
  variation moyen, courbe de Lorenz.

So the mean is something a French student must be able to *compute*; the median
is something they must be able to *read*. And "corrélation et causalité" is
named, as four words, with no elaboration whatever.

**Limits of a measuring instrument, terminale, chapter on social mobility.**

> Comprendre les principes de construction, les intérêts et les limites des
> tables de mobilité comme instrument de mesure de la mobilité sociale.

Plus a distinction the deck would recognise: that observed mobility contains a
structural component, and that "une société plus mobile n'est pas nécessairement
une société plus fluide". That is composition change masquerading as movement,
which is a Will Rogers cousin.

### What is not named

Zero occurrences, across both annexes: **biais**, **échantillon**,
**représentativité**, **expérimentation**, **ceteris paribus**, **toutes choses
égales par ailleurs**. France teaches "les techniques des sondages" without the
official text ever naming a sample or representativeness, and teaches
"corrélation et causalité" without ever naming a confounder.

## USA: the only document that names polling error in detail

Source: **AP U.S. Government and Politics Course and Exam Description, effective
Fall 2026**, College Board. Full text extracted and searched, roughly 318,000
characters. This is an examination board's framework rather than a state
curriculum, which makes it the closest structural analogue to the USMLE outline
used in the medical audit: it is what the exam is written from.

### What is required content

**Topic 4.5, "Measuring Public Opinion". LO 4.5.A: Describe the elements of a
scientific poll.** EK 4.5.A.2, quoted in full:

> Public opinion data that can affect elections and policy debates is influenced
> by polling methodology. Polling methodology is more precise when it includes:
> i. Accurate sampling methods, including calculating a margin of error
> ii. Neutral framing of questions (specific and unbiased wording of questions)
> iii. Accurate reporting (clear reporting and conclusions that can be supported
> by the data)

**Topic 4.6. LO 4.6.A: Explain the quality and credibility of claims based on
public opinion data**, turning on the "reliability and veracity of public
opinion data".

**Teaching guidance, on quantitative limitations**, names four specific ones:

> Limitations can include data from a limited demographic, opt-in polling (like
> on social media), a biased polling source, the way questions were asked, and
> more.

"Opt-in polling" is self-selection, named. That is the single most transferable
item in this whole audit and Confoundle has nothing on it.

**Gerrymandering is required content**, EK 2.3.A.2, and the CED tells teachers
students will meet "a map of a gerrymandered congressional district" as a visual
source on the exam. Note carefully what is required, though: the AP treatment is
constitutional, running through Baker v. Carr and equal protection challenges to
redistricting. It is not the votes-against-seats arithmetic. The puzzle shipped
in [#41](https://github.com/BasileChretien/Confoundle/pull/41) is therefore
complementary to what an American student is taught, rather than a restatement
of it.

### What is not named

Zero occurrences: **correlation**, **confounding**, **statistical**, **median**,
**propaganda**, **random sample**. "Bias" appears five times and never as a
technical term: twice about media bias as a contested political topic, twice in
media-literacy activities about source credibility, once in the polling
limitation quoted above.

So the American framework is deep on polling and silent on everything else.

## UK: nothing, exactly as in medicine

Source: **Politics, GCE AS and A level subject content**, Department for
Education, May 2022. This is the statutory content all exam boards must build
their specifications from. It is short, roughly 22,000 characters.

**Counts, from the whole document.**

| Term | Count |
|---|---:|
| bias | 0 |
| opinion poll / polling | 0 |
| sampling | 0 |
| margin of error | 0 |

| misinformation | 0 |
| gerrymander | 0 |
| boundary / boundaries | 0 |

The single "data" is "relevant national data sources", as material to draw on.
The word "evidence" does not appear anywhere in the document.

What the document does require is generic, and worth quoting so nobody reads the
zeros as an extraction failure:

> 20. AS and A level specifications must require students to comprehend and
> interpret political information.

> 21. AS and A level specifications must require students to critically analyse
> and evaluate the areas of politics studied in order to construct arguments and
> explanations leading to reasoned conclusions.

"Critically analyse and evaluate", with nothing named. This is the same result
the medical audit recorded for the UK MLA, from a different department, in a
different subject, six years apart. Two independent confirmations that the UK
approach is deliberate rather than accidental.

## What this means for the deck

### Three puzzles are now externally validated

- **`mean-vs-median`**. Named in France, and named with a telling asymmetry: the
  mean must be computed, the median only read.
- **`correlation-causation`**. Named in France terminale, as four bare words.
- **`more-votes-fewer-seats`** (gerrymandering, PR #41). Required content in the
  US framework. The AP teaches it constitutionally; Confoundle teaches it
  arithmetically. Complementary.

### Four gaps, each named by at least one official document

Ranked by how well they fit the deck, not by how loudly they are named. All four
now carry a source and a status in
[`lesson-backlog.md`](./lesson-backlog.md), which is the single queue.

1. **Self-selection in opinion data.** Named by the US framework as "opt-in
   polling (like on social media)". Confoundle has `survivorship` and
   `publication-bias`, which are cousins, but nothing where the respondents
   chose themselves. The classic demonstration is a straw poll with an enormous
   sample and a fatal frame, which is exactly the two-views-of-one-dataset shape
   this deck needs. **This is the strongest candidate to source next.**
2. **Polling that forges the opinion it measures.** Named by France
   ("contribue à forger l'opinion publique"). Bandwagon and underdog effects
   have an experimental literature. Harder to source cleanly than it looks,
   because the published effects are small and the outcome is usually a stated
   preference rather than a count.
3. **Margin of error and the difference between a lead and a tie.** Named by the
   US framework. Confoundle has nothing on sampling precision at all, which is a
   real hole given how much of the deck is about counts.
4. **The difficulty of measuring crime.** Named by France. This is
   `detection-bias` transplanted from medicine to policing, so it risks being a
   second name for one reasoning move, which this project has already refused
   once for attrition and once for question wording. It would need to earn its
   place by teaching something the medical version cannot.

### One decision is confirmed from outside

The medical audit already recorded that **question wording** is not a separate
lesson, because it is the framing effect again. The US framework names it as
required content ("Neutral framing of questions (specific and unbiased wording
of questions)") and files it under polling methodology rather than as a distinct
reasoning error. That is independent support for keeping it in the framing
puzzle's review bank rather than building it twice.

### The uncomfortable finding about the wishlist

None of the following appear in any of the four documents read: **Overton
window**, **the Big Lie**, **gaslighting**, **firehose of falsehood**,
**whataboutism**, **dog whistling**, **fearmongering**, **astroturfing**,
**wedge issues**. Not once, in any language, in roughly 1.2 million characters
of official text.

That is worth stating plainly rather than burying. These are media-commentary
vocabulary, not curriculum vocabulary. It does not make them bad subjects, and
the deck has already shipped `anchoring` for the mechanism underneath the Overton
window and `illusory-truth` for the mechanism underneath the Big Lie. But it does
mean that the justification which carried the medical puzzles, that a qualifying
exam somewhere requires this, is **not available** for any of them. Each has to
stand entirely on its published evidence, and each is more exposed to the charge
of being a political vocabulary lesson wearing a reasoning-puzzle costume.

The two items on that list that this audit *does* support are the two already
built. Every remaining one is unsupported by any official document read so far,
and should be held to a higher evidentiary bar than the medical puzzles were,
not a lower one.

## What has not been read

Five jurisdictions from the medical audit have no counterpart here yet:

- **Germany**. There is no national curriculum: political education is a Land
  competence, so the honest analogue is the GPJE draft national standards (an
  academic society's proposal, not binding) plus sixteen Land Lehrpläne. The
  medical audit found Germany uniquely rich in *psychological* judgement errors,
  so this one is the most likely of the five to change the conclusions.
- **Korea** (통합사회 / 정치와 법), **China** (普通高中思想政治课程标准),
  **Russia** (обществознание, FGOS and the ЕГЭ codifier), **Spain** (LOMLOE).

Two of those, Russia and China, are worth reading precisely because the medical
audit found them to be clean negatives, and a clean negative in a state civic
curriculum would mean something different from a clean negative in a medical
blueprint.

Nothing in the conclusions above depends on those five, but the ranked gap list
does, and it should be revisited once they are read.

## Method

Every count in this document comes from full-text extraction of the primary
document, searched programmatically. No count is taken from an abstract, a
summary, a commercial revision site, or a secondary description of a curriculum.
Where a term is reported as zero, the extraction was checked against a term
known to be present, so that a zero means absence rather than a broken decode.
The Japanese document required a UTF-8 extraction pass because its title block
uses an Adobe-Japan1 collection that the extractor does not resolve; the body
text decoded cleanly, which is why quotations above are drawn from the body.
